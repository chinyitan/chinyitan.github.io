Chin Yi Tan’s Personal Website

Hi there, and welcome to my website! I’m Chin Yi Tan, a DiRAC Astrophysics Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Washington in Seattle (as of Oct 2026). I grew up in Puchong, Malaysia, completed my undergraduate studies at the University of Edinburgh, and earned my Ph.D. at the University of Chicago.

My research explores the structures surrounding our Milky Way, especially its satellite galaxies. I use these tiny galaxies to investigate the nature of dark matter, the mysterious substance that shapes galaxies and the growth of structure in the Universe.

If you have questions about my research or would like to get in touch, please email me at chinyi@uw.edu.

Research

As the figure below illustrates, the Milky Way is rich in structure, surrounded by various satellite galaxies and stellar streams. My research focuses on discovering these systems and comparing their populations with predictions from cold dark matter and alternative dark matter models. Below are some of my completed projects and their accompanying papers. For a more comprehensive and up-to-date list of my publications, visit my ADS library.

DELVE Milky Way Census

My research aims to test dark matter models by comparing their predictions with the observed population of Milky Way satellite galaxies. However, our current sample is incomplete with many faint and distant systems undiscovered. A systematic census with well-characterized detection limits allows us to account for these missing galaxies and make meaningful comparisons with simulations.

To solve this issue, I have been involved using the DECam Local Volume Exploration (DELVE) survey, which aims to Dark Energy Camera (DECam) to coverage of the southern high-galactic-latitude night to search for Milky Way dwarfs . Combining DELVE with Pan-STARRS data, I conducted the largest systematic Milky Way satellite census to date, covering up to 90% of the high-galactic-latitude sky, and recovered 49 Milky Way satellites.

To quantify the census’s completeness, I tested how reliably the pipeline recovered simulated satellites injected into the survey data. Combining these detection probabilities with an empirical population model, I estimated a total of approximately 265 Milky Way satellites with MV ≤ 0 and inferred their completeness-corrected luminosity function and radial distribution.

Modelling strong gravitational lenses

I am currently building an automated pipeline to model 160 strong gravitational lenses using their HST images. The model parameters obtained can be used to determine the inner slope of the dark matter profile in elliptical galaxies and aid in the measurement of the Hubble constant.
